♟️ Chess Not Checkers — The Strategic Career Game
The operating principle: You are not picking one career and climbing one ladder. You are moving strategically across a board — each role chosen not just for its salary but for what doors it opens next. Maximum income velocity + maximum optionality at every step. This is game theory applied to your life.
// The One Rule That Governs Every Career Move
❌ Bad Chess Move
A role that pays $20k more but locks you into a narrow specialty, limits your learning, and closes doors to adjacent domains.

Example: taking a high-paying but purely administrative IT manager role that moves you away from technical depth.
✅ Good Chess Move
A role that pays $15k more AND teaches you skills that open 3 new doors, puts you in proximity to people doing frontier work, and compounds your domain knowledge.

Example: a bioinformatics analyst role at a genomics company that pays slightly less than a senior sysadmin but positions you for a $130k ML biology role 18 months later.

PHASE 4+ DESTINATION

Trajectory 2 has a research-track extension that represents the highest compensation and impact ceiling of any path in this dashboard. AI Research Scientist and Research Engineer roles at frontier labs — Anthropic, OpenAI, DeepMind, xAI — sit at the absolute top of the modern technology career landscape.

THE LANDSCAPE

Frontier AI research roles publish papers that advance the science of machine learning. They design and run experiments at massive compute scale, invent new architectures and training methods, and operate at the edge of human knowledge. Mark Chen (CRO, OpenAI), Dario Amodei (CEO, Anthropic), Demis Hassabis (CEO, DeepMind) — these are the senior endpoints of this trajectory.

COMPENSATION REALITY
→ AI Research Engineer (entry): $200,000–350,000 total comp
→ Research Scientist: $400,000–700,000 total comp
→ Senior Research Scientist: $700,000–1,500,000 total comp
→ Research Director / Principal: $1,500,000–3,000,000+ total comp
→ Chief Research Officer / Co-Founder: $5,000,000+ plus significant equity
THE CREDENTIAL REALITY

The standard path is a PhD in Machine Learning, Computer Science, Mathematics, or Physics from a top research institution (MIT, Stanford, CMU, Berkeley, Princeton, Carnegie Mellon, ETH Zurich). Published papers at NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR. Years of research apprenticeship under established researchers.

THE NON-TRADITIONAL PATHS THAT EXIST
→ Deep engineering excellence at a frontier lab that transitions into research over time (the Andrej Karpathy adjacent path)
→ Independent research output: papers in workshops, well-cited open source contributions, demonstrated research-grade work
→ Funded ML masters or part-time PhD programs while working
→ Research engineer roles that bridge engineering and research
THE HONEST READ

This is the longest, most credential-heavy, and most competitive path of any in this dashboard. From zero Python today to research-grade output: 5–8 years minimum. Frontier lab research positions receive thousands of applicants per opening. Most have PhDs from elite institutions.

But the foundation work is identical to Trajectory 2 (Engineering) for the first 2–3 years: Python, mathematics (linear algebra, calculus, probability, optimization), machine learning fundamentals, deep learning, and ability to read and reproduce research papers.

THE DECISION POINT

At roughly Year 2–3, choose between:

Engineering Path
MLOps, agent architectures, production ML systems, shipping real products. Faster to first frontier-lab role. Lower ceiling, still extraordinary.
Research Path
Theoretical depth, paper reading and reproduction, research-grade open source projects, potentially funded ML masters or PhD. Slower, higher ceiling, harder to enter.
THE STRATEGIC INSIGHT

Engineers transition into research roles regularly at frontier labs. The reverse is rare. Engineering credibility opens research doors. Pure research credentials without engineering depth are increasingly the harder sell.

For Blake's trajectory: Build the engineering foundation now. Read papers seriously starting Phase 2. Publish open-source work that demonstrates research-grade thinking. Leave this door open without making it the primary aim. The path that gets you to AI Engineer at Anthropic in 4 years is the same path that lets you transition to Research Engineer there in Year 5.

Non-CS background = 6–12 months to building real systems, not 3 years.
What to Learn Deeply vs. What to Let AI Handle
🧠 Learn This Deeply — No Shortcuts
  • Logic and control flow — if/else, loops, functions. The grammar of all programming. AI cannot think for you here.
  • Data structures — lists, dicts, arrays, dataframes. How data is organized affects everything downstream.
  • Reading and debugging code — AI writes imperfect code. You must be able to find the error and understand why it's wrong.
  • System design thinking — how components connect, what belongs where, how data flows. This is the skill that makes you the architect.
  • Math and probability intuition — linear algebra, calculus, stats. AI cannot give you this. It's the lens that makes you understand why models work.
  • Prompt architecture — how to give AI precise, structured instructions that produce the output you actually need. This is a real technical skill.
🤖 Delegate This to AI — Free Your Time
  • Boilerplate and setup code — project scaffolding, config files, imports. AI does this in seconds. Don't memorize it.
  • API syntax lookups — exact function signatures, library method names. Just describe what you want. AI knows the docs better than you need to.
  • Repetitive implementation patterns — parsing files, cleaning data, writing tests. Once you understand the pattern conceptually, AI executes it.
  • First drafts of complex functions — describe the logic you need in plain English, get 80% of the code, then review and refine.
  • Translating domain concepts into code — "write a Python function that takes a DNA sequence and counts each nucleotide" — you understand the biology, AI handles the implementation.
  • Documentation and comments — AI writes these faster and more consistently than most humans. Let it.
