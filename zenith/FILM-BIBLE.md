# Zenith Film Bible — every episode is a short film

## The rule of the show
**Everything is real except the Professor.** The science, data, history, experiments, numbers, and comparisons are real and sourced. Zenith, the Helix Institute, the Lumen, and the Helix Gate are the only fiction.

## Tone
Blunt, brilliant, and funny. Zenith dives into a subject with obvious delight, then hits the wall of what humanity currently knows and has to stop ("I'm not allowed to tell you. Yet."). That limit is the running joke and the hook. Under it sits a quiet hope: one day humanity advances up the Kardashev scale, and we meet him and his people. Use that line sparingly, about one episode in ten, so it stays special:
> "Keep climbing, students. When your species is ready, come find us."

## The look
- **Short film, never slides.** Every lesson is a physical metaphor or a real place: a building for a server, a highway for bandwidth, a real lab for a real experiment.
- **The camera always moves:** slow dolly, orbit, push-in, crane. Static shots only for comedic beats.
- **Zenith lives inside the world:** he walks, points, leans on things, reacts.
- **Labels live in 3D space:** thin floating text attached to objects. No boxes, no panels.
- **Style block (paste into every video prompt):** `Cinematic short film, stylized photoreal 3D, anamorphic lens, shallow depth of field, soft volumetric light, film grain, smooth continuous camera motion, vertical 9:16, no on-screen text.`

## The Lumen HUD (Jarvis-style, made for Zenith's eyes)
- Barely visible: thin cyan line work at 15–25% opacity, floating just above the Lumen, slightly out of focus, reflecting on his face.
- Readable if you pause: destination names, scale (10⁻¹⁰ m), coordinates. It scrolls fast because he knows exactly where he's going.
- Built by the engine as an overlay on the real footage, so the text is always correct.

## The Helix Gate (travel)
1. Zenith chooses the destination on the Lumen HUD.
2. A single vertical line of violet light appears in the air.
3. It unfolds into a tall rectangular doorway (about 1:2.3) with crackling cyan edges; the destination shimmers inside.
4. The camera follows him through. The frame washes to light and the new world resolves around us.
5. Coming home is the same in reverse: the gate opens onto the Helix Institute.

**Video prompt for the gate:** `A single vertical line of violet light appears in mid-air and unfolds into a tall rectangular doorway of light with crackling cyan edges; through it, a shimmering view of [DESTINATION]. Cinematic, volumetric, static camera.`

## Transitions are scenes
Every move from one act to the next is a mini-scene with a job: it carries an idea across the cut, so the viewer never feels a seam. Write a transition shot (T#) between every act, with its own key frame, motion, SFX, and HUD. Four kinds:
1. **The Helix Gate (place):** a doorway of light to a new location.
2. **The Time-Step (time):** Zenith steps sideways out of the moment. The world freezes mid-motion and drains of color, pages of time riffle past like a flipbook, the faint HUD reads the year, and he steps into the new moment. The world resumes in full color.
3. **The Scale Walk (size):** one continuous zoom through scales (a person → a cell → an atom, or the reverse), with the HUD counting powers of ten.
4. **The Match Cut (idea):** the last image of one act becomes the first image of the next (frozen water ripples become candlelight ripples on a wall).

## Zenith and time (canon)
- **Real science he can teach:** spacetime is one 4D fabric (Einstein); time runs at different rates with speed and gravity (GPS satellite clocks gain about 38 microseconds a day and are corrected for it); many physicists describe a "block universe" in which all moments exist; extra dimensions are a serious but unconfirmed hypothesis; Flatland shows how a higher dimension would look from a lower one.
- **The fiction (Prime Rule territory):** Zenith is a higher-dimensional being who sees our timeline like a whole book and can step into any page.
- **The rule:** in the past, he can watch but never change anything. No paradoxes, and every historical scene stays accurate. People there barely notice him (comedy fuel).
- **His line when asked how:** "Ask me again in a few centuries."

## Production path per episode (about 8–10 shots, 6–8 s each)
1. **Script and shot list:** Claude writes them, using real sources only. Blake approves.
2. **Key frames:** Nano Banana, one still per shot (Identity Block + style block + shot description).
3. **Motion:** Veo or Kling, image-to-video with each shot's motion prompt.
4. **Voice:** ElevenLabs (Zenith's designed voice), line by line.
5. **Lips:** a lip-sync tool on the talking shots only.
6. **Finish:** the engine adds the HUD, composited simulations, 3D-style labels, captions, SFX, and assembles the 9:16 master.
